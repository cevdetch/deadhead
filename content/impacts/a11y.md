---
description: "Markup that gets in the way of screen readers, zoom and other assistive technology, and the WCAG success criteria behind the harmful rules."
---

Accessibility starts in the head. The page language, the title, the viewport and the
colour scheme are declared there, and each one decides something for people using
assistive technology or adjusting the page to read it. The rules here report markup that
takes something away from them, with the
[WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) success criterion where one applies.

The harmful ones:

- [`meta/viewport-user-scalable`](/rules/meta/viewport-user-scalable): zoom disabled or
  capped below 2, which fails 1.4.4 Resize Text. The most common harmful finding in the
  [10,000 home pages](/blog/what-10000-home-pages-put-in-their-head).
- [`document/html-lang`](/rules/document/html-lang): no page language, so a screen reader
  guesses its pronunciation rules. 3.1.1 Language of Page.
- [`head/title`](/rules/head/title): no title, two, or an empty one. 2.4.2 Page Titled.
- [`document/main-multiple`](/rules/document/main-multiple): two visible `<main>` landmarks.
- [`meta/http-equiv-refresh`](/rules/meta/http-equiv-refresh): a timed redirect or reload,
  which WCAG names as failure F40 of 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable.
- [`head/viewport-missing`](/rules/head/viewport-missing),
  [`meta/viewport-value`](/rules/meta/viewport-value) and
  [`meta/color-scheme-value`](/rules/meta/color-scheme-value): a page that renders tiny on a
  phone, or light controls on a dark design.

The deprecated ones are older markup with a cost to readers: `<marquee>` and the urge to
recreate `<blink>` (2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide), `<bgsound>`, which plays with no control
(1.4.2 Audio Control), a table `summary` sighted readers never see, `longdesc`, and
`<font>`, `<big>`, `<tt>` and `<strike>`, which change how text looks without saying what
it is.

Deadhead cannot test what only a person can judge, such as whether a title describes the
page or a colour has enough contrast. These rules catch markup that is wrong whatever the
content, and none of the harmful ones is autofixed, because the repair is a value the
author has to choose.
